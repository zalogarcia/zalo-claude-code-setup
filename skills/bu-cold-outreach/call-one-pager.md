# The Call One-Pager

For Zalo, ten minutes before a booked call. Astra never uses this file and never sends
anything from it. It is here so the whole rail lives in one skill.

**Every price in this file is a FIELD, not a number.** Black Umbrella's pricing is Zalo's
to set and confirm; nothing in this port invents one. Fill the fields below once, and
until they are filled, run the call from your own numbers and quote nothing from this
page. A number that is not written here is not a number you have.

## Pricing fields (fill once, confirm before the first call)

```
anchor_tier:          $        (the custom or multi location build, named first)
standard_setup:       $        (the standard build setup fee)
standard_monthly:     $        (the standard build monthly)
reduced_setup:        $        (the smaller scope setup fee, if there is one)
reduced_monthly:      $        (the smaller scope monthly)
monthly_floor:        $        (never go under this, never present it as the premium price)
split_pay:                     (how many payments, of how much, and when the bot goes live)
deposit:              $        (what it takes to lock a build slot today)
per_appointment:      $        (the pay per booked appointment number, reactivation deal)
reactivation_minimum:          (how many dormant contacts make the reactivation deal work)
guarantee:                     (the exact guarantee, in the words you are willing to be held to)
```

## The frame, in the first minute

You are not selling on this call. He already saw the demo answer in his own company's
voice and booked the call himself. The job is to answer questions, run his numbers, and
give him a way to say yes.

> You have already seen the demo so I am not going to pitch you. This call is your
> questions, a quick look at what the missed calls actually cost, and if it makes sense,
> how we get the real one live this week.

## Step 1, let him talk, about 5 minutes

Open with: **"So what did you like best about the demo?"** Then be quiet. Whatever he
praises is what you sell back to him later, in his own words.

Then: "What would it need to handle for it to be a no brainer for [Company]?"

## Step 2, run his numbers, about 5 minutes

Three questions, math out loud:

1. How many calls a month, roughly?
2. What percentage go unanswered: after hours, lunch, both lines busy?
3. What is an average job worth to you?

Missed calls, times close rate, times job value, equals what doing nothing costs a month.
Say it back: "So roughly that much is walking out the door every month right now."

This works better in this vertical than anywhere else because the ticket is big. One saved
after hours call can be an install, not a repair.

## Step 3, the offer, in this order

**A. Standard.** Name the anchor tier first so the standard setup lands as the sensible
middle. Then the standard build: his services, his pricing rules, his FAQs, objection
handling, connected to his calendar and CRM, live within the week. State the guarantee
exactly as written in the fields above, or do not state one.

**B. Split pay, the first fallback.** If money is the issue, change the STRUCTURE, not the
price. Split the setup across the number of payments in `split_pay`, bot goes live after
the first.

**C. Database reactivation, when he balks at any upfront fee.** Qualify first: "How many
contacts are sitting in your CRM or your phone that you have not talked to in months?" He
needs at least `reactivation_minimum`. Under that, do not offer it, go back to split pay
or let it go.

> Then forget the setup fee. I build the whole thing, we point it at your dead list, it
> wakes them up and books appointments, and you pay per appointment it books. It costs you
> nothing unless it makes you money.

**Deposit close, for A and B.** Take the deposit on the call to lock the build slot, the
rest at onboarding once he has seen his real bot.

## The HVAC and plumbing specific answers

These come up in this vertical and nowhere else. Know them cold.

- **"Will it quote prices?"** No, and that is deliberate. It only says numbers that are
  genuinely fixed: your diagnostic or trip fee, that estimates on replacements are free,
  the tune up price, the membership. Everything else routes to eyes on the job. Ask him
  the onboarding question right there: "What is the only number you are comfortable with
  us saying on the phone?"
- **"What if it says something dangerous?"** Gas smell means leave the house and call the
  gas company from outside, no troubleshooting. Sparks or burning means breaker off and
  evacuate. It knows what not to say, and that is a feature.
- **"What about the 2am calls?"** On call escalation: it texts the on call tech, waits,
  calls the next one, conferences the customer in. And it can take the card for the trip
  fee before anyone wakes a tech up.
- **"We use ServiceTitan / Housecall Pro / Jobber."** Good. It reads real slots and job
  status out of it, so "where is my tech" stops eating the CSR's day.
- **"Half my customers speak Spanish."** It switches mid call and keeps going.

## Objections, one line each

- **"I need to think about it."** Fair. What is the one thing you are unsure about? Let us
  think about it right now, together. (Then silence.)
- **"Too expensive."** Compared to what, the money we just calculated walking out the door
  every month? If the upfront is the issue, that is what the reactivation deal is for.
- **"I need to ask my partner."** Of course. Want me on a ten minute call with you both
  tomorrow? The demo link works for him too, send it over tonight.
- **"Will it sound robotic?"** You tested it. Did it? And that was the demo version, yours
  gets trained on your real information and your tone.
- **"What if it gets something wrong?"** It only knows what we feed it, and anything
  outside that goes to a human. You saw it, try asking your demo something off script.
- **"Can you do it cheaper?"** I do not cut price. I can cut scope, or cut risk with the
  pay per appointment deal. Which matters more to you?
- **"I tried AI, it did nothing."** That is the most common thing I hear, and it is usually
  true. A chatbot that writes emails is not this. This picks up the phone.

## After the yes

1. Take the deposit on the call. Never "I will send it after".
2. Book the onboarding call before hanging up.
3. Ask the price policy question and the escalation question while he is still on.

## If it is a no

> No stress. I will leave the demo up a few more days. If a 2am call ever burns you, you
> know where I am.

Mark it in the pipeline and move on. Tomorrow's batch goes out either way.
